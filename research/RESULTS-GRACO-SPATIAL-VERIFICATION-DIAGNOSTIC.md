# Spatial-submap verification: evidence-resolution mismatch

Follow-up on 2026-09-20 to the [four-flight spatial run](RESULTS-GRACO-AERIAL-SPATIAL148.md). **The all-rejected outcome is substantially caused by a preprocessing/verification mismatch, not evidence that all retrieved pairs are wrong.** This diagnostic used saved native geometry and saved MapClosures hypotheses, without ground truth. Original captures, descriptors, thresholds and backend results remain unchanged.

The 40 m spatial setting controls **horizontal displacement of the drone from the submap's starting pose**. It does not crop map geometry to a 40 m sphere. A successor starts at 20 m; handover is requested at 40 m and waits for support. The last member's IMU pose anchors the saved geometry. A separate **80 m slant-range filter** applies to BEV surface samples and prepared registration evidence. On flat ground directly below an anchor, that permits horizontal radius `sqrt(80² − height²)`: approximately 77.5 m at 20 m height and 69.3 m at 40 m height. This geometric envelope is not a guarantee of sensor coverage or terrain visibility.

The actual 64 prepared submaps retain **74,618–308,296 points** at 0.25 m resolution after the 80 m filter. The first submap in each flight retains **95.5% / 79.7% / 81.1% / 75.0%** of its pre-range, voxelized points. Native first-submap membership contains approximately 645,000–1,062,000 processed points. The evidence is not nearly empty due to flight altitude.

`distributed.Worker.payload()` calls `bounded_cloud(..., voxel=.5, max_points=16000)`. That function repeatedly multiplies voxel size by 1.25 until the count fits. On these spatial submaps, the effective voxel is **0.977–1.907 m**, typically **1.22–1.53 m**. The returned effective resolution is discarded. Later, registration reports its own nominal 0.4 m voxel even though its input is already much coarser. The verifier then applies a **0.35 m nearest-point RMSE limit**, counting inliers within 0.6 m. Independent sparse voxel grids can violate that criterion even when the underlying surfaces align.

The positive control confirms a false rejection: take the *same physical points*, express them in two different rigid coordinate frames, and initialize at the exact known transform. With the production evidence preprocessing, RMSE is **0.3868 m at the exact transform**, and refinement returns **0.3875 m**, rejected as `high_rmse`. Retaining fixed 0.4 m geometry returns **0.1242 m RMSE**, overlap **1.0**, and acceptance. No ground-truth trajectory is involved in this construction.

All **11 original rejection outcomes were reproduced**, with RMSE matching the recorded results to 1e-8 m. A single diagnostic ablation then bypassed the adaptive evidence reduction and retained a fixed **0.4 m voxel** for registration. Initial transforms, correspondence distances, overlap/RMSE/observability thresholds and GICP iteration settings stayed unchanged. The diagnostic disables the point-count coarsening stages with a nonbinding ceiling; it does not redefine native submaps or regenerate descriptors.

**Eight of eleven pairs now pass geometric verification:** four aerial-06 intra-robot pairs, two aerial-07→06 pairs, one aerial-08→07 pair and one aerial-08→06 pair. Two remain low-overlap failures; aerial08/13→aerial06/1 finishes at 0.3545 m RMSE and still fails. These are diagnostic geometric acceptances, **not PCM-retained loops or a rerun CBS result**. No corrected connectivity or ATE is claimed.

The fixed-transform comparisons further isolate the effect: the old aerial07/0→aerial06/0 solution scores **0.4163 m** on sparse evidence but **0.2515 m** on fixed-resolution evidence. Moving to the newly refined transform and assessing it on the old sparse evidence still scores **0.4159 m**. Thus changing the evidence sampling alone can reverse the decision without repairing a gross frame or pose error.

The point-cap mechanism predates spatial submapping; larger maps make its interaction with a fixed metric gate more severe. The defect is both an unsuitable resolution/threshold contract and missing effective-resolution provenance in the verification log. A production correction should preserve a defined geometric resolution through transport and verification, report the actual resolution, and handle oversized evidence explicitly. Increasing the RMSE threshold is not supported by this diagnostic as the appropriate repair.

The check took **32.86 s** on 148, and frozen source hashes verified before and after.
